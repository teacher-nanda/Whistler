Step 1 · 5 min

Warm-up: faces from the past

Chapter 1 starts with a surprise meeting in a museum. Pick one question and answer it in two or three sentences. Everyone gets a turn.

Question A

Have you ever seen someone from your past by surprise, like a teacher, a neighbor or an old friend? Where were you, and what did you do?

Question B

Think of a family memory that two people tell differently. Who remembers it correctly, and how do you know?

Question C

Could you recognize a person you loved as a child after forty years? What would stay the same about them?

Or choose your own.
Step 2 · 8 min

The story in two time layers

The chapter jumps between the present day and one Friday in 1980. Keep both layers in mind.

The surpriseIn New York, Daphne and her husband Jonathan meet an old man who has been watching them. He is Eddie Triplett, her stepfather from long ago.
The woundDaphne cried when she realized who he was. She tells her sister Leda she believes she ruined Eddie’s life.
The flashbackWe go back to January 1980. Leda is sick, Eddie helps, and a night drive with Daphne ends with the car leaving the road.

Now · New York City

Daphne is 53

  1. At the MetJonathan whispers that an old man keeps looking at Daphne. He leaves the next day on what might be a long trip.
  2. “This is your stepfather”Jonathan talks to the man. Daphne knows his voice before his face: Eddie Triplett.
  3. LunchThey catch up on jobs, children and books. Eddie offers his number from a tiny notebook but will not take hers.
  4. The walk across the parkJonathan goes with Daphne to Leda’s building. She realizes he does not want her to get lost.
  5. Daphne and LedaWhy did she cry? “Because I had loved him and I had ruined his life.” They talk about the car accident.

Then · Winchester, Massachusetts

Friday, January 18, 1980

  1. Leda is sick at schoolThe nurse calls Abigail, their mother, who works at a publisher with Eddie. Eddie covers for her at the office.
  2. “Make sure it’s not appendicitis”Eddie tells Abigail how to check. Leda screams when Abigail touches her stomach, and she goes into surgery.
  3. Eddie and DaphneEddie picks up Daphne, packs bags, and they visit the hospital. Leda sleeps through it.
  4. A drive with no purposeLeaving the hospital, Eddie takes Daphne on a drive that nobody can explain later. They switch off the headlights to see the stars.
  5. The edge of the roadThe car goes off the road into the dark. Eddie’s one question: is Daphne wearing her seat belt?

The two layers meet in one sentence Daphne says in Leda’s apartment: “Because I had loved him and I had ruined his life.”

Step 4 · 10 min

Close reading: quotes and words

Take a few minutes on six lines from the chapter. Tap “Think about it” for a question. Use “Add to notes” to save a line to your notes.

If you took the time to notice the people around you, chances were you’d see some of them again in a different wing.

Opening of the chapter, at the museum

The museum has “wings,” and the chapter has two time layers. Does this sentence hint at what will happen? What does it say about noticing people?
…our hearts were forever stitched together, mine and Eddie’s.

Daphne in the elevator, right after she recognizes Eddie

“Stitched” is a sewing word. It suggests a bond that can be pulled but not easily undone. Why is the word more interesting than “connected”?
Because I had loved him and I had ruined his life.

Daphne explains why she cried

The sentence has two parts joined by “and.” Why does she put love and ruin side by side? Does it sound like a fact or a feeling?
Mom isn’t a fan of conflicting narratives. She’s married to Lucas, period.

Leda, after the museum

A “narrative” is a version of a story. What is Leda saying about the way their mother handles the past? Is Daphne also choosing her own version of the story?
Daphne had been saved, but she was always going to be a tree, a virtuous tree…

Daphne thinks about her name and her sister’s

In the myth, Daphne is turned into a tree to escape a god. Is being saved a gift, or also a limit? How does Daphne seem to see herself?
Eddie Triplett had not killed a child, nor would he ever again in his life love another person as much as he loved her.

The last lines of the chapter

The sentence first tells us what did not happen. Why start with that? Is the second half of the sentence happy, sad, or both?

Step 5 · 15 min

Let’s discuss

You do not need every question. Choose the ones your group likes. Tick a question when you have talked about it.

0 of 11 discussed

Round 1 · Understanding about 3 min

1
Who is Eddie Triplett, and how is he connected to Daphne and Leda?
Think about his job, his marriage to Abigail, how long it lasted, and why Daphne did not know him at first.
2
The chapter has two time layers. When and where is each one?
Look for the date and place that start the second layer: Friday, January 18, 1980, Winchester, Massachusetts.
3
What happened to Leda in 1980, and how did Eddie help?
Eddie’s advice was to push a little on the right side of her stomach. If she screamed, go to the ER.

Round 2 · Digging deeper about 8 min

4
Daphne “never cries,” yet she cries when she recognizes Eddie. Then the elevator doors close and the crying stops. What do you think she is feeling?
She says their hearts were “forever stitched together.” What might change when the doors shut and she is in public again?
5
Eddie felt he knew Daphne before he knew why. Daphne knew his voice before his face. How do we recognize people we have not seen for decades?
Compare the voice, the face, and the feeling. Which one lasts longest for you?
6
Daphne believes she ruined Eddie’s life, but she was only a child. Leda says, “Logic doesn’t have anything to do with it.” Can guilt be real even when it is not logical?
The narrator says, “In the way of all children, I believed the fault for their divorce was mine.” Why do children blame themselves?
7
Daphne told Jonathan that her mother was driving during the accident. Later she says she never found the right moment to correct herself. Is this a lie, a simplification, or a way to protect herself?
She says she did not tell Jonathan about Eddie when they first met, and later Jonathan became “True-Love Jonathan.” What makes it harder to correct a story over time?
8
Jonathan walks Daphne across the park, and she realizes he does it to keep her from getting lost. On the walk he also says, “Of the three, the best one got away.” What kind of man is Jonathan?
He compares her father, her stepfather and Eddie. Can you judge a person from one lunch? Why might Jonathan say this?

Round 3 · The big picture about 4 min

9
The chapter ends in the middle of the crash. Why might the author stop there, and what do you want to know next?
The last lines tell us what did not happen. How does that change the feeling of the ending?
10
About the drive, the narrator says that “there was no logic. Stop looking for it.” Have you ever done something without a reason that became an important memory?
Daphne thinks a trip like this was “the kind of thing Daphne only could have done with Eddie.” What does that say about him, and about her mother?
11
Leda was named for a girl in a myth, and Daphne for another. Daphne says she is “a virtuous tree.” Do you think names shape who we become?
Both myths are about girls in danger from gods. One is saved by being turned into a tree. How does Daphne describe herself, and how does she describe Leda?

Round 1

1. Daphne recognized Eddie the moment she saw him at the museum.

The answer is false. Jonathan noticed him first. When he asked, Daphne shook her head. She knew his voice before his face.

2. Eddie and Daphne’s mother worked at the same publisher in Boston.

The answer is true. They both worked at Houghton Mifflin, and that is where they met.

3. Daphne met Jonathan because her father was a patient at the hospital where he worked.

The answer is true. Her father was dying of melanoma in the Gloucester hospital where Jonathan worked.

Round 2

4. Leda’s appendix ruptured during surgery.

The answer is true. It took the doctor longer than expected, and Leda slept through the hospital visit.

5. Eddie and Daphne had a clear plan for their night drive.

The answer is false. No one could find a purpose for the trip. In the narrator’s words, “there was no logic.”

6. Eddie and Abigail had been married for fourteen months.

The answer is true. The marriage was short, but it was a big part of the girls’ young lives.
